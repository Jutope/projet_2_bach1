The Hot Water Fountain

The history of this monument

The Hot Water Fountain, also known as the “Mossy Fountain,” is one of the most unique jewels of Aix-en-Provence, blending thermal history, natural beauty, and urban memory.

Located at the top of the Cours Mirabeau, this fountain stands out for its rounded shape and its surface entirely covered with green moss, continuously fed by a thermal spring whose water flows at around 34 °C.

It is one of the few fountains in the city to use naturally warm water — a direct legacy of the springs exploited since Roman times, when the city was known as Aquae Sextiae. This connection to the ancient baths gives the fountain a unique heritage value.

Built in 1667, then modified in 1687 and 1734, it reflects the evolution of urban tastes and customs. Its current, almost organic appearance results from the constant action of the warm water on the vegetation that grows naturally upon it.

This mossy mantle has earned it the affectionate nickname “the big green sponge” among the people of Aix, who see it as a living landmark at the heart of the city.

The Hot Water Fountain is not remarkable for its sculptural richness, but for its distinctive atmosphere: it emits a light mist in winter, offers shaded coolness in summer, and seems to breathe in rhythm with the city.

It embodies both Provençal charm and the memory of the waters that have shaped Aix for over two millennia. Its quiet yet constant presence makes it a place of passage, rest, and contemplation — a meeting point between history and everyday life.
