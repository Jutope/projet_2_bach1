The Rotonde Fountain

The history of this monument

Inaugurated in 1860, the Rotonde Fountain is one of the most iconic landmarks of Aix-en-Provence. Located at the western end of the Cours Mirabeau, it marks the entrance to the historic city center and serves as a true urban crossroads.

Its construction coincided with the arrival of running water thanks to the Zola canal, making it a symbol of technical progress and urban beautification.

Monumental in scale, the Rotonde rises above two large circular basins and features rich sculptural decoration: lions, dolphins, swans, and children in bronze or stone adorn its edges.

At the top, three allegorical statues facing different directions represent Justice, Agriculture, and the Fine Arts, illustrating the founding values of the city and 19th-century society.

More than a simple backdrop, the fountain shapes Aix’s urban space. It links the old quarters to the more modern areas and remains a landmark for both residents and visitors.

Despite the surrounding traffic, it retains a strong visual and emotional presence, embodying both Provençal elegance and the urban ambition of its era.
