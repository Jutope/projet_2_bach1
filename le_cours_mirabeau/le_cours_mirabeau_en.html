The Cours Mirabeau

Discover the Cours Mirabeau!

Beneath the leafy arches of century-old plane trees, the Cours Mirabeau stretches like a ribbon of light between past and present.

Its fountains whisper ancient stories, while the golden façades of townhouses reflect the Provençal sun.

Here, time slows down. Café terraces buzz with conversation, passersby stroll leisurely, a book in hand or a coffee in the other.

It’s an open-air stage, where every stone, every shadow, every burst of laughter composes a scene of southern serenity.

The Cours Mirabeau is not just an avenue: it’s a breath of life, an art of living, a tribute to the tranquil beauty of Aix.

The Statue of King René!

On his white marble pedestal, good King René watches over the city he cherished.

Draped in his cape, scepter in hand, he holds a bunch of muscat grapes—a nod to the introduction of this grape variety to Provence.

At his feet, sculpted books recall his love for knowledge and the arts.

The fountain that supports him was inaugurated in 1823, on the site of an older fountain destroyed during the Revolution.

It marks the end of the Cours Mirabeau, like a gateway to the old town and the Mazarin district.

The medallions on the base pay tribute to his faithful companions: Jean Matheron de Salignac and Palamède de Forbin, figures of medieval Provence.

More than just a monument, it is a guardian figure, a historical and emotional landmark for the people of Aix. King René, who died in Aix in 1480, still inhabits the place—between legend and stone.
