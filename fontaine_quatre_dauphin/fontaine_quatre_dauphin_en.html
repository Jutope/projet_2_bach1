Four Dolphins Fountain

The history of this monument

The Four Dolphins Fountain, built in 1667 in the heart of the Mazarin district, is one of the most emblematic Baroque masterpieces of Aix-en-Provence.

Designed by the sculptor Jean-Claude Rambot for Archbishop Michel Mazarin, it symbolizes the alliance between elegance, movement, and architectural balance.

The circular basin, carved from Sainte-Baume stone, features four stylized dolphins made of Calissanne stone, their powerful bodies coiling around a central column, once topped by a fleur-de-lis, then a pine cone, and now a Maltese cross.

This succession of emblems reflects the political and religious changes of Provence through the centuries.

Placed at the center of the square that bears its name, the fountain marks the entrance to the Mazarin district, where nobility and the Provençal art of living still meet today.

Around it, ocher-colored facades, plane trees, and the murmur of water create a peaceful, timeless atmosphere, where visitors can rediscover the soul of Aix and the charm of its heritage.

Carefully restored in 2015, it remains an essential landmark of Aix’s architectural and cultural identity.
