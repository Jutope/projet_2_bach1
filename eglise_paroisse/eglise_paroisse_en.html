St. John of Malta Church

The history of this monument

St. John of Malta Church, located in the heart of Aix-en-Provence near the Granet Museum, is one of the city’s most remarkable monuments.

Built at the end of the 13th century by the Hospitaller Order of the Knights of St. John of Jerusalem, it marks the arrival of the Gothic style in Provence and stands out for its sober yet elegant architecture.

Its tall nave, ribbed vaults, and large stained-glass choir windows create a sense of verticality and light that captivates visitors upon entry. The austere façade is nonetheless adorned with sculpted details and a rose window that reflect the symbolic richness of the building.

Inside, the harmonious proportions and restrained decoration enhance the serene character of the place, while modern additions such as new stained-glass windows and a grand organ establish a successful dialogue between past and present.

The church also served funerary and political purposes, housing the tombs of figures connected to the County of Provence, which gave it significant historical importance. Its central location makes it a key heritage landmark for anyone wishing to understand Aix’s religious and urban evolution.

Visiting the church offers both a lesson in regional Gothic architecture and a moment of contemplation within a space steeped in history.
